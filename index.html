Free-first operating model

No paid API keys, no account required, and no data sent to a server. Your entries stay in this browser unless you export them.

Recommended cost-control sequence

  1. Define the mission objective before choosing hardware.
  2. Set mass, power, dimensions, frequency, and data-rate constraints.
  3. Compare launch pathways using current written quotes—not estimates alone.
  4. Confirm launch-provider requirements before buying flight hardware.
  5. Seek an eligible sponsored opportunity early; funding for building the spacecraft may still be needed.

Funding & launch opportunity finder

Official links and free engineering software. Eligibility, deadlines and availability can change; confirm details on the source website.

  • NASA CubeSat Launch Initiative (CSLI) — selected eligible U.S. educational/nonprofit CubeSat missions may receive a launch opportunity. NASA does not fund spacecraft development; selection is competitive.
  • NASA CSLI announcement and application updates — check current program timing and application details.
  • NASA SmallSat mission design tools — public and open-source engineering tools.
  • NASA GMAT (open-source mission analysis) — orbit and trajectory analysis.
  • GMAT source code on GitHub.
  • FCC satellite services — U.S. satellite communications regulatory information.
  • FCC spectrum management resources — spectrum and authorization context.
  • NASA Small Spacecraft Systems Virtual Institute — technology state-of-the-art and design resources.
  • U.S. SBIR/STTR program — research funding opportunities for eligible small businesses when solicitations are open.
  • Grants.gov — search federal funding announcements; eligibility is program-specific.

Important nonprofit note

Do not assume a nonprofit automatically qualifies for a free launch. NASA CSLI selection depends on mission relevance, eligibility, proposal quality, schedule and available launch capacity. Check current announcements before building a budget around it.
